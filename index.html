StreamingR2V

Towards Long and Interactive Reference-to-Video Generation

Anonymous Submission

Introduction

Existing reference-to-video methods render user-specified subjects faithfully, but only within a short clip whose cast is fixed before generation begins. Real shots don't work that way: subjects enter and leave partway through, and creators want to adjust the cast while the video is still unfolding. StreamingR2V opens up both limits at once. It keeps the horizon open-ended and lets references be added or removed as generation proceeds, so a subject can join or exit at any moment in a long video while the identities already on screen stay intact.

Qualitative Comparison with Previous Methods
We present qualitative video comparisons between StreamingR2V and existing baseline methods. Since streaming reference-to-video generation is relatively new, there are not many direct comparable methods. The comparable baselines include commercial models (Kling-Omni and MiniMax-H3), two-stage baselines that concatenate R2V models with SVI, and a baseline that directly tunes the SVI model on our SR2V dataset mentioned in the paper.
Qualitative comparison of general streaming reference-to-video generation. Two-stage baselines and SVI-R2V SFT suffer from severe background inconsistencies and abrupt motion changes. Although MiniMax and Kling-Omni generate fluent videos, they tend to expose the subject identity too early at the beginning, because all reference images are provided upfront.
